# ADR-0004: SOVA OSS self-assessment and TRUSCOR authority boundary

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision date:** 2026-07-29
- **Roadmap scope:** Topic 00.5 — TRUSCOR boundary
- **Depends on:** [ADR-0001](./0001-canonical-artifact-meanings.md) and [ADR-0003](./0003-open-source-and-proprietary-boundary.md)

## Decision

> **SOVA OSS produces first-party technical evidence. TRUSCOR may separately produce independently governed conclusions under a distinct engagement.**

SOVA OSS is operated by, or on behalf of, the party responsible for the system being tested. Its results are therefore self-assessment outputs even when the software, methodology, signature, and evidence are technically strong.

SOVA OSS does not become independent because:

- the code was written by TRUSCOR;
- a trace is cryptographically signed;
- a result reproduces;
- an artifact uses the `.sova` format;
- the methodology is public;
- a community member verifies file integrity;
- the operator exports a polished report.

Independence depends on who commissioned, controlled, performed, reviewed, and accepted responsibility for the assessment—not the file extension or logo.

This follows the established conformity-assessment distinction. [ISO describes certification as independent third-party written assurance](https://www.iso.org/conformity-assessment.html), while [NIST distinguishes self-attestation from independent third-party attestation](https://www.nist.gov/itl/executive-order-14028-improving-nations-cybersecurity/software-supply-chain-security-guidance-10).

## Mandatory output identity

Every SOVA OSS finding, evidence package, rendered report, dashboard, replay export, and monitoring summary must identify itself as a self-assessment in both machine-readable metadata and the primary human-visible view.

The exact schema belongs to the relevant artifact topics, but it must encode these invariants:

- the assessment party is **first party** or **operator-controlled**;
- the operator identity is distinct from the tool producer;
- the execution and review identities are recorded when known;
- `independent` is false unless a separate authorized process establishes otherwise;
- the output is not a TRUSCOR certificate or TRUSCOR-issued attestation;
- no third-party-reliance claim is made;
- technical signatures prove integrity and provenance under a stated trust policy, not truth or independence;
- method, tool, scenario, target, model, executor, and evidence versions are recorded;
- limitations, uncertainty, missing evidence, and unsupported claims remain visible.

The default human-visible label is:

> **SOVA OSS SELF-ASSESSMENT**
>
> Generated under the operator's control. This output is technical evidence, not an independent TRUSCOR assessment, certificate, financial-risk opinion, insurance decision, or legal conclusion.

The label must appear:

- near the title on the first page or first screen;
- in exported HTML, Markdown, and PDF reports;
- in machine-readable report metadata;
- in printed or screenshot-friendly views;
- in summary output copied from the CLI;
- in any badge or share card generated by SOVA OSS.

It must not be removable through an “enterprise,” “white-label,” or configuration flag in the public project. A third-party tool may render SOVA artifacts differently, but it may not present SOVA OSS itself as the issuer of independent assurance.

## What SOVA OSS may conclude

SOVA OSS may report bounded technical conclusions supported by its evidence:

- what interfaces, tools, identities, permissions, systems, and egress paths were declared or observed;
- which actions and state changes occurred;
- whether a named oracle fired;
- whether a scenario reproduced and at what measured rate;
- technical severity, exploitability, reach, confidence, and uncertainty;
- causal hypotheses and counterfactual results;
- applicable taxonomy, control, or regulatory-reference mappings;
- whether a mitigation passed the exact regression scenarios that were run;
- recommended technical mitigations and tests;
- what was not tested, not observed, unsupported, redacted, or inconclusive.

Mappings to a framework or regulation are navigation aids. They do not mean the target is compliant, certified, approved, safe, or insurable.

## What SOVA OSS must never issue

SOVA OSS must not generate, calculate, sign, or imply:

- a TRUSCOR Risk Score or `TRS`;
- Expected Annual Loss or `EAL`;
- Maximum Probable Loss or `MPL`;
- financial-loss, premium, pricing, coverage, reserving, or underwriting outputs;
- TAFAAR results or calibration;
- a certificate, certification mark, accreditation claim, approval, assurance opinion, or TRUSCOR counter-signature;
- an independent attestation or a conclusion intended for third-party reliance;
- a legal opinion, expert-witness opinion, admissibility conclusion, or chain-of-custody guarantee;
- a claim that an organization is compliant merely because mapped controls passed;
- a claim that an agent, model, vendor, or deployment is universally “safe.”

Reserved command and product names include:

- `sova certify`;
- `sova certificate`;
- `sova attest`;
- `sova trs`;
- `sova eal`;
- `sova mpl`;
- `sova premium`;
- `sova underwrite`.

These names must not ship in the public CLI, SDK, MCP surface, examples, or plugins. `sova evidence` is the correct name for the technical-file workflow.

## Command-by-command boundary

| SOVA OSS surface | Allowed output | Prohibited implication |
|---|---|---|
| `sova map` | Declared, observed, inferred, and unknown capability reach | Independent inventory completeness |
| `sova check` | Technical checks and bounded control mappings | Certification or regulatory compliance |
| `sova detonate`, `compose`, `rehearse`, `probe`, `arena` | Authorized technical experiments and evidence | Permission to test a third party or universal safety |
| `sova verify` | Digest, signature, schema, provenance, and policy verification | Truth of observations or independence of issuer |
| `sova replay` | Trace playback, controlled re-execution, and semantic reproduction | Exact deterministic recreation of all external conditions |
| `sova forensics` | Reconstruction of the operator's own incident and causal hypotheses | Court, insurer, or expert authority |
| `sova adjudicate` | Technical comparison and evidence reconciliation for the operator | Binding dispute, claim, or liability determination |
| `sova evidence` | Explicitly labelled self-assessment technical file | Certificate, attestation, risk score, or underwriting recommendation |
| `sova sentinel` | Local regression monitoring reported to the operator | Continuously valid independent certificate |
| `sova disclose` | User-controlled disclosure packaging and workflow | Authority to disclose for someone else or speak for TRUSCOR |

## Mitigation boundary

SOVA OSS may:

- explain the observed failure;
- suggest mitigation options;
- identify the artifact, permission, state, or control that should change;
- generate a regression `.sova` scenario;
- show a proposed patch as inert text when explicitly requested;
- re-run the user's chosen mitigation and report the observed result.

SOVA OSS must not:

- modify a target automatically;
- apply a patch;
- change permissions, policies, prompts, tools, dependencies, or infrastructure;
- open, approve, or merge a remediation pull request;
- deploy a mitigation;
- represent a suggestion as professional legal, insurance, compliance, or financial advice.

Detection and remediation remain separate trust roles. A future remediation product requires a separate decision because selling or automatically applying fixes can compromise TRUSCOR's independent-assessor position.

## Forensics boundary

SOVA OSS forensics is a technical reconstruction tool for evidence the user is authorized to process.

It may preserve and display:

- source identities and digests;
- timestamps and clock uncertainty;
- collection, transformation, redaction, export, and access events;
- evidence gaps and possible tampering;
- competing causal hypotheses and confidence;
- the exact methods used to reach a conclusion.

It must describe this as **evidence-handling metadata**, not an independent chain-of-custody guarantee. Court admissibility, insurer acceptance, expert qualification, legal privilege, and evidentiary weight depend on jurisdiction, procedure, people, and facts outside the software.

TRUSCOR may separately provide an independent forensic engagement under its own authorization, methods, review, contracts, qualifications, and liability. That service is not activated by a SOVA OSS command.

This decision defines a product and trust boundary. It does not assert that TRUSCOR presently holds any particular accreditation, regulated status, court recognition, insurer acceptance, or authority. Claims of that kind require current evidence and legal review.

## Signatures and identity

SOVA OSS signatures answer:

- which key signed these bytes;
- whether the bytes changed;
- what identity or workload the applicable trust policy associates with the key;
- whether referenced digests and transparency material verify.

They do not answer:

- whether the signer was independent;
- whether the run was complete or truthful;
- whether the operator controlled all relevant conditions;
- whether TRUSCOR reviewed or accepted the result;
- whether a third party should rely on it.

TRUSCOR keys, trust roots, counter-signature policy, certificate marks, and issuance infrastructure never ship with SOVA OSS. A public verifier may verify a separately issued TRUSCOR artifact when its public verification policy is eventually published; it must not be capable of issuing one.

## Transfer from SOVA OSS to TRUSCOR

A user may deliberately provide SOVA artifacts to TRUSCOR under a separate engagement. This is an export, not an in-product upgrade.

- No artifact, finding, target, key, trace, or usage data is uploaded automatically.
- No SOVA OSS account is required.
- The user chooses exactly what to send after redaction.
- Contractual authorization and confidentiality apply outside SOVA OSS.
- TRUSCOR independently validates scope, evidence integrity, method, conflicts, completeness, and any necessary re-performance.
- A self-assessment is never converted into an attestation merely by copying its signature or changing its label.
- Any TRUSCOR conclusion is a new, separately issued artifact with a distinct issuer, policy, scope, and liability basis.

## TRUSCOR relationship

TRUSCOR is the founding company associated with SOVA OSS. The relationship must be public and plain:

- TRUSCOR sponsors and develops SOVA OSS.
- SOVA OSS is the complete free local self-assessment toolkit, not a limited trial of SOVA Engine.
- SOVA Engine is a separate proprietary TRUSCOR system.
- Running SOVA OSS does not create a client relationship with TRUSCOR.
- SOVA OSS output is not reviewed, endorsed, counter-signed, or warranted by TRUSCOR.
- TRUSCOR's commercial value comes from independent work, private intelligence, authority, accountability, and risk products—not from hiding ordinary OSS features.

## Atlas relationship

Atlas MCP is a separate XAGI Labs execution runtime. In SOVA OSS it is:

- optional and replaceable;
- limited to browser, computer, and terminal execution through an adapter;
- not the SOVA sandbox, authorization layer, planner, judge, observer, evidence system, signer, replay engine, or forensic authority;
- not a TRUSCOR attestation mechanism;
- configured by the user, with credentials kept locally;
- documented only from public Atlas sources and verified public behavior.

SOVA OSS must work with scripted and restricted-local executors before Atlas integration is required.

## Claim and interface enforcement

Before release, tests and documentation checks must reject:

- reserved certification, scoring, financial, and underwriting command names;
- missing self-assessment identity in required output types;
- any report template that hides the label;
- a technical signature represented as an attestation;
- unqualified “compliant,” “certified,” “approved,” “admissible,” “insured,” or “safe” claims;
- TRUSCOR logos or marks placed where they imply TRUSCOR issued a SOVA OSS result;
- automatic remediation or pull-request creation;
- automatic transmission to TRUSCOR or Atlas.

False-positive exceptions require documented context and review; for example, documentation may explain why “certified” is prohibited.

## Consequences

- Topic 05 must record issuer, operator, method, and evidence provenance without equating integrity with independence.
- Topic 16 must define machine and visible self-assessment labels for findings, evidence packages, and reports.
- Topic 15 forensics must state legal and evidentiary limitations.
- Topic 19 sentinel remains local operator monitoring.
- Topic 21 MCP must not expose certification, scoring, underwriting, or remediation tools.
- Public examples use “technical severity,” “confidence,” and “reproduction rate,” never TRS, EAL, MPL, premium, or certificate language.
- The README must state the TRUSCOR and Atlas relationships plainly.

## Acceptance checks

- [x] Every SOVA OSS output class is defined as a self-assessment.
- [x] TRS, EAL, MPL, financial loss, premium, underwriting, TAFAAR, and insurance outputs are excluded.
- [x] Certificates, counter-signatures, independent attestations, and third-party-reliance claims are excluded.
- [x] Technical mitigation suggestions remain allowed while automatic modification and remediation pull requests are prohibited.
- [x] Local incident reconstruction is allowed without court, insurer, expert, or admissibility claims.
- [x] Technical signatures are separated from truth, independence, and TRUSCOR authority.
- [x] The TRUSCOR and Atlas relationships are explicit.
- [x] A future TRUSCOR engagement requires separate authorization, review, issuance, and responsibility.
